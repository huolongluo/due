**Project Description**

The model can underwrite. It cannot advance until Attestcoin cites the payment.

Quay Factors files invoice 4417: Lin Wei is owed 12,000 qUSD for LED drivers sold to Kestrel. Kestrel paid on Ethereum Sepolia. Reed, an AI credit officer running on Creditcoin, may read the file. It may not mint. `DueVault.cite` submits merkle and continuity proofs to the Attestcoin native verifier at `0x0FD2`, then the official EvmV1Decoder extracts `InvoicePaid` from the same bytes. The log must come from the registered `InvoicePay`, the token must match, the receipt must have succeeded, and the encoded transaction cannot be replayed. Only then `advance()` mints a 90% DUE advance to Lin. A pasted Etherscan screenshot is a claim. Reed lists it under ignored. The vault stays closed.

This expands Creditcoin’s user base: Ethereum payers, Creditcoin settlement, for a real trade-finance job.

**Attestcoin Protocol Integration Summary**

Attestcoin is the credit file. The worker waits until CC3 attests the Sepolia block (`ProofBuilder.waitUntilHeightAttested`, chainKey 1), then `getProof`. `DueVault.cite` calls `verifyAndEmit` on precompile `0x0000000000000000000000000000000000000FD2`. On success it decodes the proven transaction with the official decoder at `0x731c345d79Fb8BbDC541f9DF3b6317585F849F9f`, selects `InvoicePaid(uint256,address,address,uint256)`, checks emitter/token/invoice id, and records `citedAmount`. `usedTx[keccak256(encodedTransaction)]` blocks replays. `advance()` does not take a proof; it only reads what citation already bound. Depth of use is the scoring criterion we optimized for: verification, decode, business-event match, replay — not a wrapped token bridge.
